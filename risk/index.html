Read this before committing anything

What you
are risking

Signing the declaration risks nothing. Committing money is a different decision, and this page is the part nobody enjoys writing.

Don't commit money unless you are prepared for its value to fall, possibly by a lot, with no way to act while it is committed.

That is the honest version. There is no compensation scheme behind this, no protection if it goes wrong, and nobody to complain to.

This is not an investment

You are not buying a share, a token, or a right to anything. There is no return and there is never going to be one — not a dividend, not a profit share, not a payout of any kind beyond your own money being released to you unspent.

If any part of you is hoping to make money here, you have misread what this is, and you should not take part.

Bitcoin falls as well as rises

It has halved before and it will again. If you commit money and the price drops, the money you committed is worth less — and because it is locked, you cannot do anything about it until your lock ends.

That is the deal, and it is the price of a commitment the blockchain can enforce rather than one everybody has to be trusted to honour. Bear markets have lasted two and three years, which is longer than a one-year lock.

So: do not commit money you might need, and do not commit money you could not bear to watch fall by half. Pick your lock length with that in mind rather than to look impressive on a register.

You are responsible for your own money

Nobody holds your coins, which is the great strength of this design and also means nobody can help you if you lose access to them.

If you lose the key to your wallet, your bitcoin is gone. There is no password reset, no account recovery, no support line, and no technical trick that brings it back. Not us, not anybody.

If you have never held bitcoin before, learn how backups work properly before you commit anything. Practise with a small amount first. The movement will still be here.

It costs money to take part

Two separate costs, and the first is the bigger one:

Renewing a lock when it expires£1.50–£7 againBitcoin miners, not us

None of it comes to this movement. We take nothing, charge nothing and earn nothing from your participation.

But it does mean small amounts are poor value. On £100, fees might be eight to thirteen per cent of what you commit. On £500 they are a couple of per cent. Fewer, larger commitments cost far less than many small ones — and a five-year lock costs one fee where five annual locks cost five.

It may simply fail

Nobody has done this before. Any one of these ends it:

We never raise enough to buy anything meaningful.

No owner is ever willing to sell a stake to a body like this.

Football's regulators decide a movement with anonymous members cannot hold a stake in a club.

A solicitor finds a flaw in the legal structure that cannot be worked around.

Interest fades and nobody renews their lock.

In every one of those cases the outcome is the same: locks run to their dates and every supporter's money is released back to them, unspent. There is no pot to lose, no company to go bust and no administrator to appoint, because at no point was your money anywhere but your own wallet.

You would have tied up some money for a while, got it back, and ended up with nothing to show for it but the attempt. That is the realistic worst case, and it is worth being clear-eyed that it is also the most likely one.

Risks in the software

The code holding the locks has been written and tested, and tested code is not the same as safe code.

At the time of writing, nobody outside this project has reviewed it. A mistake in how a lock is built could in principle make coins unspendable — not stolen, but stuck. Before this movement asks anyone to commit a meaningful sum, that code needs eyes that are not ours, and anyone considering a large commitment should wait for that.

Everything is published so that it can be checked rather than believed. That is not the same as it having been checked.

Tax is yours

In the UK, spending bitcoin on something counts as disposing of it, and a disposal can have capital gains consequences. So if your locked bitcoin goes into a purchase, that is yours to account for.

Getting your money back is not. Leaving a lock moves coins from one address you control to another address you control, which disposes of nothing. Selling that bitcoin for pounds afterwards is your own separate decision and nothing to do with this fund.

We do not collect your details, so we cannot and will not report anything on your behalf. Keep your own records.
