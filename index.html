The Supporters' Declaration

Liverpool Football Club belongs to its supporters in every way except ownership. This is an attempt to fix a piece of that: to buy a real stake in the club with money put up by fans, held by nobody, and directed by the people who put it in.

We are supporters raising money to buy a stake in Liverpool Football Club, and to hold it on behalf of supporters. Anyone, anywhere, with any amount, and without telling us who they are.

There is no pot. All money stays in the wallet of the supporter who put it there, and nothing can move it without that person's own signature. Nobody — not an organiser, not a committee, not whoever wrote this — ever holds anybody else's money.

The principles
  1. The purpose is to buy and hold a stake in Liverpool Football Club on behalf of its supporters. Not to trade, not to invest, not to build a business. One club.
  2. Money in, glory out. No money is ever paid out to any member, in any form, for any reason. If the fund ever owns part of Liverpool FC, what Liverpool FC earns stays with Liverpool FC.
  3. Nobody holds anybody else's money. Each supporter's contribution remains in a wallet only they control, and returns to them on a date they chose themselves, whatever becomes of this movement.
  4. Only committed money is counted. Bitcoin a supporter merely owns does not count towards anything. Neither does support they merely feel. What we claim, we can prove.
  5. What a member receives is a say, and never a return. The society that holds the stake will be bound by its own rules to vote the club's shares the way its members vote — not to consult them, not to consider their views, but to vote as they voted, on the public record.
  6. Money decides how money is spent. People decide how the club is run. Spending is weighted by what each member has committed, because it is their money. The club's direction is one member, one vote, because a wealthy supporter is not more of a supporter.
  7. There is no founder's share. Nobody receives weight, priority or payment for having arrived first.
  8. No member has to say who they are. A wallet is the only credential this movement asks for, and no database of supporters will be built.
  9. No app is the only way in. The rules of this movement are published documents, not software. Anyone may write their own app, or use an ordinary wallet and no app at all, and be treated exactly the same.
  10. No figure is published that cannot be checked by a stranger against the public record, including the figures that count against us.
  11. Anyone who disagrees with where this goes can take their money and go their own way — and until a stake is actually bought, that costs them nothing but their place in the queue.

Sign it with the key that holds your bitcoin, and you are a member. Members are numbered in the order their signatures arrive.

No money moves when you sign. Committing comes later, if and when you choose.

Signing it

Signing is not paying. You agree to the words above by signing one line of text with a bitcoin wallet. It costs nothing, moves no money, never touches the blockchain, and commits you to no payment ever. All it proves is that the holder of one bitcoin address read this and agreed to it. Putting money behind it is a separate decision, made later or never.

This is the line, and it carries this declaration's fingerprint — which is what stops anyone ever claiming you agreed to different words:

I agree to the Supporters' Declaration v1 (sha256:3a0a0bca96ec2161d98ff9da05ae885d6b8d68e55ed412bc85dcc7fb16209b96) and join as a member.

Copied. Paste it into your wallet exactly as it is.

How many have signed, and how much is committed, is not written here on purpose. Those numbers are on the register, where they are counted from the blockchain and from published signatures your own browser re-checks. A figure typed onto this page would be a claim; a figure counted there is a fact. Expect the real one to be small.

Nobody has put a penny into this and nobody has been asked to, which remains the most important untested thing about it. This is a very early thing: some documents, some working code that nobody outside the project has reviewed, and a handful of people at most. If that sounds like the beginning of something rather than the end of it, that is because it is.

This is not an investment. There is no return, no dividend and no payout, now or ever. Bitcoin rises and falls, and money you commit can fall in value while it is committed. Nothing here is legal or financial advice.
