Real money behind your word

Lock

Locking sends bitcoin to an address that only you can open, on a date you choose. Nobody can move it in the meantime. This page works out that address for you; the bitcoin goes there from any wallet you already use, and no app is needed.

Not open for real bitcoin yet

This page can be tried now on the test Crowdlock, whose coins have no value: add ?c=kop-fund-testnet4 to the address in your browser. Nothing here touches real money until the way home has been proved for the wallets people actually use, and this page says which those are.

Test network

This Crowdlock runs on a test version of bitcoin. Its coins are free, worthless and cannot be bought. Use it to find out how this works; do not send real bitcoin to anything on this page.

4 Tell the register

    
A lock address is just a hash. Nobody can tell it is yours, or that it counts, unless you say so. Publishing it adds your lock to the register once the bitcoin has one confirmation, and it counts towards the total.

    
This is public, and permanent. It ties this address to your membership, so anyone can see that you have money locked and how much. The register can't hold a lock privately. Don't publish if you would rather not.

    
You can do this before or after sending. The register keeps looking until the money has arrived and confirmed.

Taking it home

    
On the date, the key you signed the declaration with can move the money to any address you choose, with nobody else involved. Until then, nobody can.

    
What has been proved, and what has not. Making this address and sending to it works from any wallet, because sending to an address needs no special support. Taking the money out needs your wallet to sign for a custom script, and that is where wallets differ.

    
    
      
  • The Crowdlock app's own wallet: proved on a test network.
    • 
      
  • A Ledger: in progress. The Crowdlock now publishes what a Ledger needs, but taking a lock home with one has not yet been proved end to end.
    • 
      
  • Sparrow, Electrum and others used directly: not tested. We do not know that they can sign for this script, and will not say they can until it has been tried.
    • 
    

    
This is why the page is closed for real bitcoin: a lock that cannot be taken home is the one mistake that cannot be undone. When a way home has been proved for a wallet, this page will say so beside that wallet's name before it opens.

    
Every lock goes home on its date by itself in the sense that nobody can stop it. Whether you have a tool that can sign for it is what has to be true first. The script, the keys and the date are all public, so any future tool can do it.

  


  

    
Before you put money behind it

    
The risks page is short, specific and not reassuring. Read it before, not after. Bitcoin you lock is out of reach until its date except through a purchase you have personally signed for. In many countries sending, receiving or moving bitcoin has tax or reporting consequences; the rules differ and finding them out is yours to do.
